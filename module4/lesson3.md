**Product Development Process (Michael Seibel's Guide)**

## Common Mistakes to Avoid (The "Old Way")

- **Lack of Documentation:** Holding product meetings without writing down decisions or specs.
- **Building Too Big:** Attempting to build "fully formed" products instead of MVPs (Minimum Viable Products).
- **Neglecting Data:** Failing to spec analytics before launch, leading to an inability to measure performance.
- **Stagnant Roadmaps:** Creating months-long development cycles that kill team excitement and prevent iteration.
- **Founder Dictatorship:** Making product decisions in a non-transparent process without team input.

## The Development Cycle Framework

- **Define Cycle Length:** Determine a timeframe based on your platform (e.g., two weeks for iOS, shorter for web) to keep the team excited and ideas fresh.
- **Focus on a Single Goal:** Focus every cycle on one of three metrics:
  - Increasing content creation.
  - Increasing new users.
  - Increasing retention.
- **Assign a Product Lead:** The lead's job is to protect the cycle, moderate meetings, and ensure every team member feels heard.

## The Brainstorming & Selection Process

- **Judgment-Free Brainstorming:** List all ideas on a whiteboard under three categories: New Features, Maintenance, and A/B Tests.
- **Technical Grading:** Have engineers grade each idea by difficulty:
  - **Easy:** Multiple tasks can be done in a day.
  - **Medium:** Takes about half a day.
  - **Hard:** Takes most of the development cycle (must be broken down if it exceeds one cycle).
- **Build Consensus:** Select tasks transparently based on the current goal and the "cost" (difficulty) of the idea. This prevents "pet projects" and bullying from dominant personalities.

## Execution & Measurement

- **Detailed Specs:** Every task must have a clear spec and a "Success Measurement" (specific analytics to track).
- **No Analytics, No Launch:** Never release a feature without the tools to measure its impact.
- **Prioritize Ruthlessly:** Separate "need to haves" from "nice to haves."
- **Delete the Roadmap:** Don't maintain a long-term roadmap. Start each cycle from scratch using new data, user testing insights, and the current goal.

## The Work Cycle & Quality Control

- **Silent Execution:** Once the cycle begins, engineers work uninterrupted on well-defined tasks while the Product Lead handles ops, data digging, and user testing.
- **Monthly User Testing:** Conduct in-person sessions to gather qualitative insights.
- **Shared Testing Burden:** Dedicate the last three days of the cycle to manual testing. The entire team participates to ensure quality and share the "burden" of finding bugs.

## The Result

- **Rapid Iteration:** Even if the original vision changes, this process allows a team to ship a high volume of features and growth experiments quickly.
- **Team Harmony:** Efficient execution reduces founder conflict and maintains high team commitment and morale.
