**Finding and Working with Co-Founders**

- **The Importance of Co-founders:** Having a co-founder increases productivity, provides moral support, and ensures accountability. Data shows that of YC's top 100 companies, 96% had multiple founders.

- **Where to Look:** Start with your personal network (classmates, colleagues, friends). If that fails, use platforms like YC's Co-founder Matching, which facilitates "speed dating" and filtered searches.

- **The "Non-Technical" Strategy:** If you are a non-technical founder, you must differentiate yourself by "bragging" about your achievements and showing tangible progress (like an MVP or user base) to attract technical talent.

- **Avoid Dev Shops:** Non-technical founders should avoid hiring "dev shops." They are expensive, move too slowly for the iteration needed in a startup, and lack the mission-driven care of a co-founder.

- **Alignment on Values:** Before committing, have "uncomfortable" conversations regarding financial runway, salary needs, expected work hours (weekends/evenings), and long-term motivation.

- **Trial Projects:** Before signing legal papers, engage in a 2–4 week "trial project" with a specific goal. This is the only way to see if your working styles actually mesh under pressure.

- **Equal Equity Splits:** YC recommends splitting equity equally (or near-equally). Since most of the work is ahead of you, unequal splits often lead to resentment and demotivation over the 7–10 year journey.

- **Decision-Making Structure:** Avoid "consensus-only" decision-making, which leads to gridlock. Assign clear titles (naming one CEO) and define specific areas of ownership where one person has the final say.

- **Trust and Psychological Safety:** Build a "trust by default" culture. Spend time together in person to lower communication friction and create a safe environment where mistakes are normalized rather than blamed.

- **Maintenance Habits:** Foster the relationship through regularly scheduled 1-on-1s and bi-directional feedback. Learn each other's stress responses (e.g., "attack" vs. "retreat") and practice the "disagree and commit" philosophy.
