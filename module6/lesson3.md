**Startup Growth (Gustaf Alströmer, YC Partner & Former Airbnb Growth Lead)**

## Phase 1: The Early Days (Pre-Product Market Fit)

- **Do Things That Don't Scale:** Borrowing from Paul Graham's philosophy, founders must engage in manual, unscalable activities to get off the ground (e.g., selling door-to-door or manual outreach).
- **The Airbnb Example:** In the early days, founders acted as "undercover" photographers, visiting hosts in New York to take professional photos and interview them personally to identify bugs and friction points.
- **Founders Drive Growth:** In the beginning, growth doesn't happen through ads or "launching" to a waiting world; it happens because founders manually recruit their first users and friends.

## Phase 2: Measuring Product-Market Fit (PMF)

- **Retention is the Best Metric:** PMF isn't a feeling; it is measured by unbiased data showing repeat usage over time.
- **Defining the Core Metric:** Identify the "value" event and its natural frequency.
  - **Airbnb:** Bookings (Annual).
  - **Instagram:** Viewing photos (Daily).
  - **Gusto:** Running payroll (Monthly/Bi-weekly).
  - **Lyft:** Riders taking trips (Weekly).
- **The Retention Curve:** Plot the percentage of users who return over time.
  - **Good Product:** The curve eventually flattens out (e.g., 20-30% of users stay forever).
  - **Bad Product:** The curve continues to drop toward zero.
- **"Vanity" Metrics to Avoid:** Registered users, total visitors, and Net Promoter Scores (NPS) are often misleading and do not prove PMF.

## Phase 3: Growth Channels & Tactics (Post-PMF)

**Prerequisite:** Do not focus on scaling growth until you have retention. Pouring users into a "leaky bucket" is a waste of resources.

**Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO):**
- Map out your funnel (e.g., Homepage → Search → Listing → Booking).
- Optimize specific friction points: Internationalization (translation), Authentication flows, Onboarding, and Purchase conversion.

**Primary Growth Channels:**
- **Search (SEO/SEM):** Best for "rare behaviors" (e.g., looking for a doctor or insurance).
- **Referrals/Virality:** Best when word-of-mouth is already happening. Use financial incentives to amplify it.
- **Sales:** If you can list your customers (e.g., B2B), don't use ads—just call or email them directly.
- **Paid Marketing:** Only do this if you have revenue and a high Lifetime Value (LTV). Your LTV must be higher than your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).

## Phase 4: Decision Making at Scale

- **A/B Testing:** Once a company is large enough, use A/B tests to decide between designs or features rather than relying on the "loudest voice in the room."
- **Trust Data Over Instincts:** Even experienced founders often have "bad" instincts.
  - **Example:** Airbnb found that forcing a "signup wall" actually increased bookings by 2.6% on iOS, even though it seemed like high friction.
- **The "Alternative History" Tool:** A/B testing creates two parallel universes to prove exactly what caused a metric to change, which is essential for making decisions with a large team.

## Conclusion Summary

- Start by doing things that don't scale.
- Verify PMF by looking for a flat retention curve.
- Scale using one or two primary growth channels once you have a "sticky" product.
- Decide using data and experimentation as the team grows.
