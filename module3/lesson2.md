**Avoiding "Rookie Mistakes" with Co-Founders**

- **Document Everything Early:** Don't rely on memory or verbal agreements. Write down equity percentages and vesting schedules immediately to prevent disputes when the relationship is later tested by stress.

- **Prioritize Relationship over Skill Match:** A common error is choosing a partner based solely on a specific skill set. Skills can be learned on the job, but a poor personality match or an inability to work together is a fatal flaw for a startup.

- **Pressure-Test the Relationship:** Before committing, ensure the relationship has survived disagreements. Founders who have never fought often "blow up" during the first real crisis because they haven't developed a conflict-resolution process.

- **Understand Stress Responses:** Learn how your co-founder reacts to pressure—whether they attack or retreat. Understanding these patterns allows for better interpretation of their actions and prevents unnecessary escalations.

- **Master the Art of Fighting:** Avoid "fighting dirty" or conflict avoidance. Founders must learn how to disagree honestly and come out of arguments "better and wiser" rather than letting resentment build in silence.

- **Recognize When to Break Up:** If you haven't had a real conversation with your co-founder in weeks or months, a breakup is likely inevitable. Delaying the split only makes it more destructive, leading to litigation and "cap table" damage.

- **Structure Equity for Decisiveness:** While near-equal equity is recommended to ensure mutual investment, a 50/50 split can cause deadlocks. A "pro tip" is for the CEO to have one extra share to act as a tiebreaker in a stalemate.

- **Find the Team Before the Idea:** It is better to find a co-founder first and develop the idea together. This creates a sense of "collective ownership" rather than a late-addition co-founder feeling like they are just an employee of your company.

- **Don't "Slack" on the Search:** Finding a co-founder is one of the most important tasks in the early stages. Treat it with 100% effort rather than an afterthought, as a great co-founder is a "superpower" for surviving the pre-product-market fit stage.

- **Front-Load the Effort:** Investing time to set up the relationship correctly at the start is much easier than trying to "do over" a failed company. Most founders regret not spending more time choosing the right person and setting the foundation properly.
