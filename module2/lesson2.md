**Paul Graham's Essay: How to Get Startup Ideas**

- **Don't "think up" ideas; notice them:** The best ideas are "organic"—they grow naturally out of the founder's own life and experience. Trying to manufacture ideas often leads to "made-up" or "sitcom" ideas that sound plausible but solve no real problem.

- **Solve your own problems:** Working on a problem you personally have ensures that the problem actually exists. It prevents the most common startup mistake: building something nobody wants.

- **Aim for the "Well":** It is better to build something that a small number of users want a lot (narrow and deep) than something a large number of people want a little (broad and shallow). It is much easier to expand a deep niche than to make a shallow product deeper.

- **Live in the future:** Position yourself at the leading edge of a rapidly changing field. When you "live in the future," the things that are missing from the world become obvious to you.

- **Turn off the "Schlep Filter":** Many great ideas are ignored because our brains unconsciously filter out tasks that seem tedious, messy, or difficult (the "schlep"). Some of the best startups, like Stripe, succeeded because they were willing to tackle a boring but necessary problem.

- **Turn off the "Unsexy Filter":** Do not ignore ideas just because they aren't "cool" or "sexy." Solving a despised but necessary problem (like e-commerce in the 90s) is often more lucrative than chasing trends.

- **Build "toys":** Many billion-dollar companies (Apple, Microsoft, Facebook) began as "toys" or hobby projects. If you build something "cool" that people love using, it may be more important than it initially seems.

- **Advice for students:** Instead of taking "entrepreneurship" classes, students should focus on becoming experts in a field, learning to build things, and working on interesting projects with other smart people.

- **Don't fear competition:** Being "late" to a market is often a good sign because it proves demand. You only need a "beachhead"—a specific group of users who urgently need your version because the incumbents are overlooking something.

- **The ultimate recipe:** If you want to find the best startup ideas, the most effective long-term strategy is: "Live in the future and build what seems interesting."
