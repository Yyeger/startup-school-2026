**Business Models and Pricing (Aaron Epstein, YC Group Partner)**

## The 9 Business Models of Billion-Dollar Companies

**The Framework:** Almost every billion-dollar company follows one of nine proven models. Founders should copy these rather than trying to reinvent how money is made.

**The Models:**
- **SaaS (Software as a Service):** Cloud-based subscription software (Monthly/Annual).
- **Transactional:** Facilitating transactions and taking a cut (e.g., Fintech).
- **Marketplaces:** Two-sided platforms connecting buyers and sellers.
- **Hard Tech:** High-barrier technical innovations (e.g., aerospace, self-driving).
- **Usage-Based:** Charging based on consumption.
- **Enterprise:** Large-scale contracts with long sales cycles.
- **Advertising:** Monetizing user attention (requires massive scale).
- **E-commerce:** Selling physical goods online.
- **Bio:** Biotechnology and life sciences.

## Insights from the YC Top 100 Companies

- **The Big Three:** SaaS (31%), Transactional (22%), and Marketplaces (14%) account for 67% of YC's top 100 most valuable companies.
- **The Power Law:** 50% of the value in the Top 100 list comes from just the top 10 companies.
- **Marketplace Dominance:** Marketplaces make up 5 of the top 10 (e.g., Airbnb, DoorDash). While hard to start (the "chicken and egg" problem), they create "winner-take-all" outcomes due to powerful network effects.
- **The Flow of Funds:** Transactional businesses (e.g., Stripe, Brex) outperform because they sit directly in the flow of money, making them critical infrastructure that is hard for customers to remove.
- **Predictability:** SaaS is the most common model in the Top 100 because recurring revenue allows for predictable growth and compounding.
- **The Rarity of Ad Models:** Advertising only accounts for 3% of the list. These businesses only work if you become a top-10 site on the internet with extreme organic virality.

## What to Avoid (Models that rarely scale to $1B)

- **Consulting/Services:** Low margins and scales with people, not software.
- **Affiliate Models:** Too far from the actual transaction; small commissions with delayed payouts.
- **Hardware:** High capital requirements and low margins.
- **Platform-Dependent:** High "platform risk" where the host platform can shut you down or steal your revenue.

## Core Business Lessons

- **Retention is Everything:** You cannot scale a "leaky bucket." A 5% difference in monthly retention (95% vs 90%) can result in having nearly double the customers at the end of a year.
- **Moats:** Build defensibility through network effects, high switching costs (lock-in), technical innovation, or economies of scale.

## 5 Key Pricing Insights

- **You Must Charge:** Don't be afraid to ask for money. Charging is the fastest way to learn if you are building actual value and which customer segments are most serious.
- **Price on Value, Not Cost:** Avoid "Cost Plus" pricing. Price based on the perceived value to the customer (e.g., money made, costs saved, risk avoided).
- **You Are Likely Undercharging:** Most startups price too low. High prices signal high value; low prices can signal a cheap or unreliable product. Higher margins also allow you to spend more to acquire customers.
- **Pricing Isn't Permanent:** You can (and should) raise prices over time as the product improves. You can "grandfather in" early users or give them advanced notice.
- **Keep it Simple:** Complexity creates friction. Avoid confusing pricing tiers; keep plans clear and easy to understand (e.g., GitLab vs. Quicken).

## The "Segment" Story

The founders of Segment initially feared charging and asked for only $120/year. Customers were actually worried the price was too low to trust them with data.

After being forced to ask for $120,000 in a meeting, they eventually settled on $18,000—a 150x increase over their original price—proving that most founders vastly underestimate what their software is worth.
