**Paul Graham's Essay: Should You Start a Startup?**

- **Too young:** You are old enough if you've stopped "flaking" on hard tasks and can handle criticism without acting like a child.

- **Too inexperienced:** The fastest way to get experience is to start a startup; a regular job just makes you "tame."

- **Not determined enough:** Determination is the best predictor of success. If you already work on your own projects, you likely have it.

- **Not smart enough:** Most startups require hard work, not genius. If you're smart enough to worry about it, you're smart enough.

- **Know nothing about business:** Focus on the product. Making something people want is hard; the business side is easy to learn as you go.

- **No cofounder:** This is a real problem. A startup is too hard for one person, so find a partner before you begin.

- **No idea:** Ideas change anyway. Solve a problem you personally have and the business will evolve from there.

- **No room for more startups:** There is no limit to human needs. As long as people want things, there is room for new companies.

- **Family to support:** This is a legitimate risk. If you have a family, it is safer to join an existing startup or consult.

- **Independently wealthy:** If you don't need the money, only do a startup if you love the work and want to work with interesting people.

- **Not ready for commitment:** A startup takes 3–4 years, but a regular job takes just as much time with far less freedom.

- **Need for structure:** If you need someone to tell you what to do, you aren't built for a startup.

- **Fear of uncertainty:** Expect failure, but know that even a "failed" startup looks better on a resume than a corporate job.

- **Don't realize what you're avoiding:** Corporate jobs are boring and restrictive. Startups offer autonomy and genuine work.

- **Parents want you to be a doctor:** Parents are often outdated and overly cautious. Seek the success they want for you via a modern path.

- **A job is the default:** The 9-to-5 job is a recent invention. We are moving toward a future where "living by your wits" is the norm again.
