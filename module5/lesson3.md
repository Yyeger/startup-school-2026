**Do Things That Don't Scale (Paul Graham's Essay)**

## 1. Manual User Recruitment

- **Go to the users:** You cannot wait for users to come to you; you must manually recruit them one by one.
- **The "Collison Installation":** Named after Stripe's founders, who didn't just send a link for their beta but physically took a potential user's laptop and set up the software on the spot.
- **Compound Growth:** Founders often ignore manual recruitment because the numbers feel small, but a 10% weekly growth rate (starting with just 100 users) leads to millions in two years.
- **Case Study:** Airbnb founders went door-to-door in New York to recruit hosts and help them improve their listings.

## 2. Obsessive Focus on "Delight"

- **Insanely Great Experience:** In the early stages, the experience of being a user should be "insanely great," even if the product is buggy or incomplete.
- **Unscalable Attention:** Use your small size as an advantage. Big companies cannot send hand-written thank-you notes (like Wufoo did) or visit users at home; you can.
- **Feedback Loops:** Engaging directly with early users provides the best possible feedback to refine the product.

## 3. The "Contained Fire" Strategy

- **Narrow Markets:** Start in a very small, specific niche to reach a critical mass quickly.
- **Case Study:** Facebook began exclusively at Harvard, then expanded to other colleges before opening to the general public.
- **Early Adopters:** Focus on the users who need the product most and will sign up quickest (often other startups).

## 4. Manual Operations & Consulting

- **"Pulling a Meraki":** Hardware startups should consider assembling devices themselves (like Pebble) to learn about the manufacturing process and iterate designs faster.
- **The Consultant Model:** Treat a single user as a "mold." Build exactly what they need as if you were their consultant, then generalize the solution for others.
- **"Be" the Software:** Manually perform tasks that you plan to automate later. Stripe manually signed users up for merchant accounts behind the scenes to provide an "instant" experience.

## 5. Common Pitfalls & Misconceptions

- **The "Big Launch" Fallacy:** Founders often over-rely on a massive PR launch. In reality, successful startups take off gradually, not like a projectile.
- **Partnership Trap:** Partnerships with big companies rarely work for early growth; they are often more work than they are worth.
- **Shyness and Laziness:** Founders often hide behind code to avoid the fear of rejection inherent in sales and manual recruitment.

## 6. Framework: The Startup as a "Vector"

**Two Dimensions:** A startup idea should not be viewed as just "what to build," but as a pair:
- **The Product:** What you are building.
- **The Unscalable Act:** The specific manual effort you will use to get the company moving.

**DNA:** The unscalable efforts used to start—such as aggressive acquisition and customer delight—often become a permanent, positive part of the company's culture.
