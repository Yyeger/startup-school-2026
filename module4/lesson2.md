**How to Build an MVP**

## 1. The Core Philosophy of an MVP

- **The "Midwit" Meme:** Both the beginner and the expert "Jedi" founder reach the same conclusion: launch quickly and iterate. Only the "middle-tier" founder gets stuck in over-analysis.
- **Launch to Learn:** You only start truly learning about your users once a product is in their hands. Surveys and interviews are helpful but cannot replace real-world usage.
- **Don't Over-prepare:** Avoid the trap of doing hundreds of interviews, hiring large teams, or fundraising for a year before having a product.

## 2. Pre-Launch Startup Goals

- **Speed to Market:** Get the product out as fast as possible.
- **Customer Interaction:** Talk to your initial users to see if the product actually helps them achieve their goals.
- **Iterative Cycle:** Talk to users → iterate the product → repeat. After 3–6 iterations, the product will look very different and much more useful.

## 3. Overcoming the "Fake Steve Jobs" Fear

- **The Fear of Failure:** Founders often fear that a "crappy" product will kill their company. In reality, if a customer hates it, you can just improve it and reach back out later.
- **Target Early Adopters:** These users expect bugs. They use new software because they have a real problem, not because the software is perfect.
- **The Steve Jobs Myth:** People forget that the first iPhone had no App Store, no video recording, and slow 2G internet. Even the greatest products start with limited, flawed versions.

## 4. Characteristics of Successful Software MVPs

Successful MVPs (like Airbnb, Twitch, and Stripe) share three traits:
- Fast to build.
- Limited functionality.
- Appealed to a small, specific set of users.

**Real-World Examples:**
- **Airbnb:** Launched with no payment system, no map view, and only worked during specific conferences. Users had to sleep on actual air mattresses.
- **Twitch (Justin.tv):** Started with only one streamer (the founder) and no gaming content.
- **Stripe:** Originally called "Slash Dev Payments." It had no bank deals; the founders had to manually file paperwork for every new user at night.

## 5. The "Hair on Fire" Analogy

- **The Problem:** If someone's hair is on fire, they don't need a perfect hose; they will buy a brick and hit themselves on the head with it just to smother the flames.
- **The Strategy:** Your MVP is the "brick." It's not a perfect solution, but a desperate customer with a major pain point will use it anyway.
- **Target the Desperate:** Focus on users who need the solution now, rather than trying to please casual users.

## 6. Why Surveys are Limited

- **Expertise Gap:** Customers are experts in their problems, but they are not experts in solutions.
- **The Founder's Job:** It is your responsibility to build the solution. A survey can highlight pain, but only a product can test a solution.

## 7. How to Build an MVP Quickly (Tactical Steps)

- **Set a Strict Deadline:** Give yourself a window of two weeks to a month and a half.
- **Write Down the Spec:** List all the features you think you need.
- **Cut the Spec:** Review the list and remove everything that isn't absolutely essential for a desperate customer.
- **Don't Fall in Love:** Do not get attached to your MVP. Fall in love with the user and their problem instead.

## 8. Final Advice

- **Quality over Quantity:** It is better to have 100 people who love your product than 100,000 who just "kind of like" it.
- **Do Things That Don't Scale:** Manually recruit and care for your first users to understand their needs deeply.
