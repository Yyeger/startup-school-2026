**How to Get Your First Customers (Gustav Alströmer, YC Group Partner)**

## 1. The Core Mindset: Do Things That Don't Scale

- **Manual Effort is Required:** Startups do not take off by themselves; founders must manually recruit their first users.
- **The Code Trap:** Many founders wrongly believe that writing more code or improving the product in isolation will lead to growth.
- **The Startup Curve:** Most companies go through the "Trough of Sorrow" and "Wiggles of False Hope." Only founders who stay the course and manually drive sales reach Product-Market Fit.

## 2. Why Founders Must Do Sales

- **Customer Intimacy:** Sales and talking to users are two sides of the same coin. You can't know what to build if you don't know how to sell it.
- **DNA of the Company:** Sales shouldn't be outsourced early on. Founders must learn it first to know what "good" looks like before hiring a team.
- **Authority & Passion:** Founders are the ultimate experts on their product. Their passion for solving the problem is more infectious and effective than a hired salesperson.

## 3. Tactics for Effective Outreach

**The Perfect Cold Email:**
- **Length:** Short (6–8 sentences max).
- **Format:** Plain text only (no HTML/marketing templates).
- **Content:** No jargon; address a specific problem; include "social proof" (why you are impressive).
- **Assets:** Link to a simple website with screenshots or a very short video/GIF.
- **CTA:** End with a clear ask for a meeting or a call.

## 4. Managing the Sales Funnel

**The Process:** Prospecting (making a list) → Outreach (emails/LinkedIn) → Demo → Closing → Onboarding.

- **Don't Forget Onboarding:** If you don't manually help early users get set up, they will churn immediately because early products are usually difficult to use.

**Prioritization:**
- Focus on the easiest customers first (people you know, then other startups).
- Avoid big companies early on; they have too much bureaucracy.
- Identify "early adopters." Most people are not early adopters; don't waste time trying to convince skeptics.

## 5. The Importance of Charging

- **Value Validation:** If a customer won't pay, they aren't a real customer, and you don't have a real company.
- **Avoid Free Trials (B2B):** Instead of a free trial, offer a money-back guarantee or a month-to-month contract. This ensures skin in the game.
- **Pricing:** Increase your price until customers complain but still pay.

## 6. Sales as a Numbers Game (Funnel Math)

- **Work Backwards:** To get 2 customers, you might need to do 10 demos, which requires 20 responses, which requires 500 cold emails.
- **The Common Mistake:** Founders often send 100 emails, get zero customers, and conclude "sales doesn't work." In reality, they simply didn't do enough volume to overcome the natural drop-off.
- **Use a CRM:** Track every stage of the funnel to identify where you are losing people.

## 7. Recommended Tools & Resources

- **Tools:** Apollo.io (leads), Hunter.io (emails), Close.com or Pipedrive (CRM).
- **Books/Blogs:** Founding Sales by Pete Kazanjy and Lenny's Newsletter.
- **Strategy:** Don't start with scalable channels (SEO/SEM) too early. They are expensive and prevent you from talking directly to your first 100 users.
