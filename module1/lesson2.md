**FAANG Jobs vs. Startup Founder Path**

- **Founder vs. Employee Mindset:** Technical founders often mistakenly follow career advice meant for people who want to be lifelong managers or employees. The FAANG path is optimized for stability and status, which is often the opposite of what a founder needs.

- **The "Scale" Myth:** Recruiters often pitch FAANG jobs as the only place to work on "hard technical problems at scale." In reality, many engineers end up working on mundane tasks like minor ad server tweaks or UI buttons that may never ship.

- **The Need to "Unlearn":** Big companies have specialized internal tools and heavy processes. Founders often find these skills irrelevant to a startup, where they must build everything from scratch. You often have to "unlearn" the high-overhead corporate way of building products.

- **Diminishing Returns for VCs:** While having a FAANG name on your resume provides some initial validation for investors, staying too long (e.g., 8+ years) can become a negative signal, suggesting you may have lost the "scrappiness" required for a startup.

- **The Retention Trap (Golden Handcuffs):** Big tech companies use "dark patterns" and loss aversion psychology. By issuing rolling equity grants that vest over four years, they ensure there is always a significant amount of "unvested money" you feel you are "losing" if you quit.

- **Lifestyle Inflation:** Working at a FAANG company often leads to "upscaling" your lifestyle (nicer cars, expensive vacations). This increased personal burn makes it much harder to leave and live on the lean budget required to start a company.

- **Talent Warehousing:** Large companies often hire top talent simply to keep them away from competitors. These engineers are sometimes placed in "moonshot" groups that are eventually axed, resulting in years of work that never reaches the public.

- **Psychic Scars:** Working on projects that feel ethically questionable or useless can destroy a developer's optimism. To be a successful founder, you need to maintain a level of optimism that corporate bureaucracy often drains.

- **The Low Risk of Quitting:** For talented engineers, the risk of leaving is lower than it feels. If a startup fails, you can almost always return to a FAANG job. The "spell" of the job is often harder to break than the actual job market is to navigate.

- **Plan Your Exit at the Start:** To avoid getting trapped, have a clear plan before you sign the contract. Know exactly what you want to get out of the job (e.g., a visa, a specific amount of savings, or a resume boost) and set a firm departure date before the "bonus cycle" begins.
