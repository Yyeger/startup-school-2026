**KPIs and Prioritization for Early-Stage Startups (Divya, YC Partner)**

## The Core Definitions

- **KPI (Key Performance Indicator):** Metrics used to measure if your work is actually functioning. They provide internal and external accountability.
- **Prioritization:** The process of deciding the order of tasks. Since time is finite and a founder's work is never done, prioritization determines how you and your team spend your most valuable asset: time.

## Choosing the Right KPIs

- **Primary KPI = Growth:** For the vast majority of startups, the primary metric should be Revenue Growth.
- **Exceptions:** Marketplaces (GMV/Signups), early Enterprise (Letters of Intent), or Hard Tech/Biotech (Technical milestones).
- **Secondary KPIs (3–5 max):** Metrics that ensure you aren't "cheating" your primary KPI. These include:
  - Retention and Churn.
  - Unit Economics (ensuring you aren't "buying" growth at a loss).
  - Payback Period (how quickly a customer pays back their acquisition cost).
- **Avoid Vanity Metrics:** Be wary of metrics that boost your ego (LinkedIn likes, conference invites, press) but don't move you closer to Product-Market Fit (PMF).

## The Prioritization Framework

- **Identify the Top KPI:** Determine the single most important number to move.
- **Identify the Bottleneck:** Be honest about what is stopping that number from growing (e.g., Super Daily realized their bottleneck wasn't the app UI, but the lack of a specific milk brand).

**The Ranking Process:**
- Write down all ideas to hit the goal.
- Rank by Probability of Success.
- Sub-rank by Complexity/Time (how long it takes to execute).
- **Execute and Retro:** Pick a few tasks, work on them, and if the KPI doesn't move, ask "Why?" repeatedly until you find the root cause.

## Common "Fake Progress" Traps

Smart founders often fall into these traps because they feel productive but don't actually move the business forward:

- **Low-Leverage Tasks:** Optimizing paperwork, legal structures, or incorporation details beyond what is "good enough."
- **Premature Optimization:** Building for scale you don't have yet or "beautifying" features no one is using.
- **Passive Activities:** Taking coffee meetings with investors when not fundraising or attending conferences.
- **Perfectionism:** Agonizing over decisions. It is better to make a "pretty good" decision quickly and fix it later than to be paralyzed by indecision.

## Setting Growth Targets

- **The YC Standard:** 5–7% week-over-week growth is "good"; 10% is "exceptional."
- **Compounding Matters:** Early growth is significantly more valuable than late growth because of the compounding effect.

**Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up:**
- **Top-Down:** Set a goal (e.g., $5k MRR) and work backward to find the required weekly growth rate.
- **Bottom-Up:** Look at what you can realistically achieve next week and project outward. Use both to find a balance between ambitious and achievable.

## Key Strategic Advice

- **Focus on Upside:** Don't spend all your time on "downside protection" (fixing minor bugs). Focus on "chasing upside" (finding out what makes users use the product every day).
- **Talk to Users:** This is the only way to know what to build. Iterate based on feedback, not on what is intellectually "hard" or "cool" to solve.
- **Charge Money Early:** Don't wait to charge. Paying customers provide fundamentally different feedback than free users (Reference: Scribd lost 90% of users when they started charging but finally built a real business).
- **Fail Fast:** If an idea isn't working, move on quickly. Indecision is a silent killer of startups.

## Immediate Action Items (The "Homework")

- Define your primary and secondary KPIs.
- Set ambitious weekly targets.
- Audit your current task list: remove anything that isn't the biggest blocker to your primary KPI.
- Share these goals with a community or co-founder to stay accountable.
